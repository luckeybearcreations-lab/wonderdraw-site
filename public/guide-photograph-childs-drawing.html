How to photograph your child’s drawing

The finished art can only be as good as the photo it starts from. You do not need a scanner or a camera. A phone and a window are enough.

1. Lay it flat

Put the drawing on a table or the floor. If it has been folded or rolled, press it under a heavy book for a few minutes first. A curled corner throws a shadow and bends the lines.

2. Use daylight, not the flash

Set up beside a window during the day. Flash and overhead bulbs leave a bright glare spot, especially on crayon and marker, and it wipes out the color underneath. Bright shade is better than direct sun.

3. Shoot from straight above

Hold the phone flat over the middle of the page so the edges of the paper look parallel to the edges of the screen. A photo taken at a slant makes the drawing wider at one end, and the characters stretch with it.

4. Keep your shadow out

If your hands or the phone shade the page, turn so the window is to one side of you instead of behind you.

5. Fill the frame, but keep every edge

Move in until the paper nearly fills the screen. Leave a thin border of table showing on all four sides so nothing your child drew gets cut off. Send the whole drawing, not a crop of the best part.

6. Tap to focus and check the faint lines

Tap the middle of the drawing on your screen, hold still and take two or three shots. Zoom in on the result. Light pencil and yellow crayon are the first things to disappear, so make sure you can still see them.

7. Send it as it is

No filters, no edits, no color correction. The wobbly lines and the purple sky are the point. JPG, PNG, WEBP and HEIC files up to 15 MB all work, which covers a normal phone photo.

Then tell us the story

A sentence or two helps more than you would think: who is in the picture, what they are doing, and anything that must not change. If the green shape is the family dog, say so.
