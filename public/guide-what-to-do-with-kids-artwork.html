What to do with the pile of kids’ artwork

It starts as one drawing on the fridge. Then it is a stack on the counter, a folder from school and a bag in the closet. You do not have to keep all of it, and you do not have to feel bad about that. Here is a simple way through the pile.

1. Give new art one landing spot

Pick a single place where every new drawing goes: a tray, a basket or one drawer. The goal is only to stop the pile from spreading. When the spot is full, that is your signal to sort, not to find a bigger basket.

2. Sort on a schedule, not every day

Deciding the fate of each drawing the day it comes home is tiring, and everything feels precious that afternoon. Wait. Once a month, or at the end of each school term, spread the stack out on the floor. A few weeks of distance makes the favorites obvious.

3. Make three piles

Keep: the ones that make you stop. A first family portrait, a drawing with a story attached, anything your child is especially proud of.

Photograph, then let go: pieces you like but do not need on paper. A photo keeps the memory and takes no shelf space.

Let go: worksheets, coloring pages, duplicates and half-finished scribbles. Recycling these is not a judgment on the artist.

4. Let your child choose some

Ask your child to pick a few favorites from the stack. Their choices are often not the ones you would make, and that is worth knowing. It also turns sorting into something you do together instead of something that happens while they are out.

5. Write on the back before you forget

For everything in the keep pile, pencil a name, the date and one line about what it shows on the back. “The dog at the beach, with a hat.” In a few years that sentence will matter as much as the drawing.

6. Photograph the middle pile

Lay each piece flat near a window and shoot from straight above with your phone. Put the photos in one album so they stay together. Our guide on how to photograph your child’s drawing covers light, angle and framing in more detail.

7. Store the keepers flat

One flat box or portfolio per child is enough, labeled with their name and the year. Keep it somewhere dry and out of direct sun, since marker and construction paper fade in light. When a box fills up, sort it again rather than starting a second one for the same year.

8. Put the rest to work

Paper art does not have to go straight to the recycling bin. A few easy second lives:

Rotate a display. Hang a string with clips or use one frame that opens from the front, and swap in a new piece every week or two.

Send it to family. Grandparents, aunts and uncles are usually glad to get an original in the mail with a short note.

Use it as wrapping paper or cards. Big paintings wrap small presents well. Smaller drawings fold into cards.

Make a year-end book. Print the photos from step 6 into a simple photo book, one per year.

9. Make something special from one favorite

Every so often a drawing stands out from the rest. That one can become more than a page in a box. Frame it, or have it turned into finished art. A note on the guilt

Keeping a small, chosen set is a way of honoring the art, not dismissing it. A box of twenty drawings you can actually look through is worth more than a closet of hundreds nobody opens.
